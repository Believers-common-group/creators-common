## 0. Release disposition

This release establishes the first controlled definition of Creators Common for:

- repository and platform architecture;
- creator, creation, contribution and licence data models;
- programme and guild formation;
- evidence, versioning and provenance workflows;
- public-site and registry development;
- integration planning across Believers Common, DigitalMe, Synnergyze, RiverOS, Warden, EmpireOS and the Virtual Silk Road.

This release shall not be represented as:

- a patent, design, trademark or copyright registration;
- a legal assignment or transfer of intellectual-property rights;
- professional certification or laboratory accreditation;
- regulatory, clinical, diagnostic or product approval;
- proof that an unvalidated scientific or engineering claim is true;
- permission to perform an activity prohibited or restricted by law.

## 1. Canonical definition

**Creators Common is the governed creation, contribution, provenance and economic-rights registry of the Believers Common ecosystem.**

It exists to ensure that every meaningful creation can answer:

1. Who conceived, designed, engineered, validated, improved and preserved it?
2. What evidence supports its stated capabilities and history?
3. Which controlled version is currently being used?
4. Who owns, governs, licenses, manufactures, operates or preserves it?
5. Who is entitled to attribution, compensation and continuing participation?
6. Under what conditions may it be reproduced, modified, manufactured, deployed, distributed or retired?

Creators Common preserves continuity from the first recorded idea through concept definition, prototype, verification, validation, production, licensed deployment, network use, improvement, supersession and long-term preservation.

## 2. Foundational doctrine

Creators Common adopts the following doctrine:

> Creators remain connected to what they create. Creations remain connected to their evidence. Every authorised use remains connected to its responsibilities and value distribution.

The doctrine creates four inseparable relationships:

- **creator to contribution** — a person or institution is credited for the work actually performed;
- **creation to evidence** — claims remain linked to designs, tests, decisions and limitations;
- **version to use** — every deployment identifies the controlled release on which it depends;
- **use to obligation** — permission, restrictions, reporting, compensation and safety duties travel with the licence.

## 5. Creator and contributor roles

Creators Common shall distinguish authorship, ownership, sponsorship, execution, review, validation and custodianship.

Recognised roles may include:

- originating creator;
- co-creator;
- concept contributor;
- design contributor;
- engineering contributor;
- research contributor;
- software contributor;
- data contributor;
- manufacturing contributor;
- validation authority;
- domain reviewer;
- safety reviewer;
- institutional sponsor;
- funding sponsor;
- owner;
- custodian;
- commercial operator;
- improvement creator;
- derivative creator;
- preservation steward.

A sponsor does not become an author merely by funding work. An owner does not become the sole creator merely by holding legal rights. A project leader does not automatically absorb the contributions of the team.

## 6. Creator Passport

A Creator Passport is the portable, controlled identity record of a person or institution participating in creation.

It may contain:

- verified identity reference;
- public display identity;
- disciplines and capabilities;
- institutional affiliations;
- registered contributions;
- creation portfolio;
- reviewer and validation roles;
- training and certifications;
- licences and permissions;
- commercial participation;
- lawful restrictions or suspensions;
- evidence-backed reputation indicators.

Reputation shall be based primarily on verified work, review quality, fulfilled obligations and evidence—not popularity alone.

## 7. Creation Passport

A Creation Passport is the controlled identity of the creation itself.

Minimum fields should include:

| Field | Required function |
|---|---|
| Creation ID | Permanent machine-readable identity |
| Creation name | Human-readable title |
| Creation class | Scientific, engineering, digital, design, cultural or organisational |
| Purpose | Defined problem and intended use |
| Originating creators | Initial authorship and conception |
| Contributors | Role-specific contributions |
| Owner | Legal or contractual rights-holder, where applicable |
| Custodian | Entity responsible for continuity |
| Technical definition | Controlled specification or reference |
| Evidence references | Designs, tests, validations and decisions |
| Maturity | Current C0–C8 stage |
| Version | Current controlled release |
| Parent and derivative links | Provenance and lineage |
| Classification | Public, member, confidential, restricted or sealed |
| Licence | Permitted use and conditions |
| Economic allocation | Attribution, royalties and participation |
| Limitations | Known boundaries, exclusions and unresolved risks |
| Status | Draft, active, superseded, suspended, withdrawn or preserved |

A public registry may disclose identity, ownership, maturity and status without exposing protected sequences, formulas, source code, drawings, personal data or trade secrets.

## 8. Contribution Record

Every material contribution should be capable of receiving a Contribution Record containing:

- contributor identity;
- creation and version affected;
- role and precise description of contribution;
- date or development interval;
- evidence references;
- reviewer or acceptance status;
- confidentiality level;
- attribution terms;
- economic entitlement;
- licences or assignments granted;
- disputes, corrections or supersession history.

Contribution Records shall not be silently deleted merely because ownership, employment or project leadership changes.

## 9. Creation maturity lifecycle

### C0 — Idea registered

- problem or opportunity described;
- originator recorded;
- timestamp and preliminary evidence preserved;
- no claim of technical feasibility implied.

### C1 — Concept defined

- intended purpose established;
- initial architecture or method described;
- contributors and sponsor roles identified;
- risks and dependencies preliminarily recorded.

### C2 — Prototype created

- initial working embodiment produced;
- prototype evidence captured;
- limitations and failure modes recorded;
- prototype is not represented as production-ready.

### C3 — Verified

- technical requirements defined;
- creation shown to perform stated functions under defined conditions;
- verification evidence attached;
- defects and exceptions documented.

### C4 — Validated

- intended use and users defined;
- fitness for intended use evaluated;
- performance boundaries and limitations established;
- independent or designated validation authority identified where required.

### C5 — Production-ready

- controlled specification completed;
- build, manufacturing or deployment package prepared;
- release, supplier, configuration and quality controls established;
- service and maintenance obligations defined.

### C6 — Licensed deployment

- approved parties may manufacture, deploy, operate or distribute;
- licence obligations become enforceable;
- evidence-return and reporting duties are active;
- monitoring and suspension pathways exist.

### C7 — Network creation

- creation is interoperable across affiliated nodes;
- common evidence, quality and identity rules apply;
- network performance and compatibility are monitored;
- local variations remain version-controlled.

### C8 — Preserved creation

- long-term custodian assigned;
- source and evidence archives completed;
- dependencies and knowledge-transfer requirements recorded;
- continuity, restoration and succession procedures established.

Maturity shall be claim-specific. A creation may be C5 for one application and only C2 for another.

## 10. Licence families

Creators Common may support the following configurable licence families.

### CC Open Knowledge

For public learning, attribution-based use and governed open collaboration.

### CC Research

For controlled academic, experimental and research use.

### CC Commercial Use

For integration into commercial services, products or business processes.

### CC Manufacturing

For physical or digital production according to a controlled specification.

### CC Deployment

For operation at approved customer, laboratory, factory, field or infrastructure nodes.

### CC Network

For use across affiliated Virtual Silk Road, Synnergyze or programme nodes.

### CC Derivative

For modification, adaptation or improvement while preserving lineage and registering the derivative.

### CC Restricted

For confidential, safety-critical, biological, defence-related, regulated, dual-use or otherwise controlled creations.

Every licence may define:

- territory;
- duration;
- field of use;
- permitted users and roles;
- usage or volume limits;
- evidence and reporting obligations;
- confidentiality and security controls;
- royalty and fee rules;
- derivative rights;
- prohibited uses;
- inspection or audit rights;
- suspension, withdrawal, expiry and renewal rules.

## 11. Attribution and economic participation

Attribution and economic rights are independently configurable.

A participant may receive:

- attribution only;
- a fixed creation or development fee;
- milestone payments;
- unit royalties;
- revenue share;
- usage-based royalties;
- licence-fee participation;
- derivative or improvement royalties;
- network participation allocations;
- stewardship or preservation allocations.

Potential recipients include:

- originating creators;
- co-creators and contributors;
- improvement and derivative creators;
- sponsors and funders;
- validators and reviewers;
- manufacturers;
- operators and distributors;
- custodians;
- registry, evidence and preservation functions.

No universal percentage applies to every creation. Allocation must be declared in the applicable agreement and recorded against the relevant Creation Passport and Licence Record.

## 12. Evidence and version control

Every controlled creation should support:

- immutable or tamper-evident historical versions;
- source and release artefact hashes;
- evidence references;
- author and reviewer identity;
- creation, approval and release timestamps;
- change rationale;
- dependency and compatibility records;
- known limitations and unresolved risks;
- supersession and withdrawal notices;
- deployment references showing which version was used.

Corrections are permitted through controlled change. Historical records must not be rewritten to conceal error, dispute, failure or earlier authorship.

## 13. Classification and access

Records or artefacts may be classified as:

- **Public** — openly discoverable and accessible;
- **Member** — available to recognised ecosystem members;
- **Confidential** — restricted to named parties and roles;
- **Restricted** — subject to enhanced policy, safety or legal gates;
- **Sealed** — held by a designated authority and not ordinarily accessible.

Classification must be applied to the smallest necessary unit. A Creation Passport may be public while detailed formulas, code, sequences or drawings remain restricted.

## 14. Governance boundaries

Creators Common shall not:

- declare an unverified idea to be a working invention;
- declare a prototype to be validated without evidence;
- convert research-use work into diagnostic or regulated use by labelling alone;
- expose restricted biological, technical, security or personal information;
- treat sponsorship as authorship;
- erase earlier contributors when ownership or leadership changes;
- detach derivatives from their source lineage;
- permit commercial use without the required licence;
- allow a registry entry to be presented as legally required approval;
- suppress material limitations, adverse evidence or known safety issues.

## 15. Disputes and corrections

Disputes may concern:

- authorship;
- contribution scope;
- ownership;
- licence interpretation;
- evidence integrity;
- maturity classification;
- derivative lineage;
- attribution and economic allocation;
- misuse or unauthorised deployment.

A controlled dispute process should preserve:

- the disputed record;
- positions and evidence of each party;
- temporary restrictions where necessary;
- reviewer or forum decisions;
- corrections and appeals;
- final resolution and continuing obligations.

## 16. Guilds and programmes

Creators Common may form discipline-specific guilds and programmes. Each must define:

- scope and terminology;
- creator and reviewer roles;
- minimum passport fields;
- maturity gates;
- evidence requirements;
- safety and misuse controls;
- licence classes;
- validation and release authorities;
- interoperability requirements;
- preservation obligations.

The first controlled implementation is:

> **Creators Common → Molecular Sciences Guild → qPCR Creator Programme**

## 17. Change control

Changes to this canon shall include:

- new release number;
- change summary;
- author and reviewer identities;
- rationale;
- affected sections;
- migration requirements;
- compatibility impact;
- approval status.

A future release may supersede V0.2-R0, but the historical release shall remain accessible.

## 18. Canonical statement

> **Creators Common is the governed home of creators and their creations. It preserves identity, contribution, provenance, evidence, version history, licensing and economic participation from the first idea through validation, production, deployment, improvement and long-term preservation.**
